How Does Currency Exchange Actually Work?

Currency exchange is the process of converting one country's money into another's. The rate at which this happens — called the exchange rate — tells you how much of one currency you get for one unit of another.

For example: if the USD to PKR rate is 278, it means one US dollar buys 278 Pakistani rupees. If you have 1000 dollars, you multiply by 278 to get 278,000 rupees.

Exchange rates are not fixed by governments in most countries. They float — meaning they change constantly based on supply and demand in global currency markets, which trade 24 hours a day, 5 days a week. [Source: International Monetary Fund]

The global foreign exchange (forex) market trades approximately $7.5 trillion per day — making it the largest financial market in the world by volume. [Source: Bank for International Settlements]

Why Do Exchange Rates Change Every Day?

Exchange rates move constantly because they reflect the balance of supply and demand for each currency. Several factors drive these changes:

  • Inflation: When a country has high inflation, its currency tends to lose value relative to currencies with lower inflation. Pakistan's rupee has weakened significantly against the dollar in recent years partly due to higher domestic inflation. [Source: State Bank of Pakistan]
  • Interest rates: Higher interest rates attract foreign investment, which increases demand for a currency and strengthens it.
  • Trade balance: Countries that export more than they import tend to have stronger currencies because foreign buyers need to purchase the local currency to pay for exports.
  • Political stability: Political uncertainty causes investors to move money out of a country, weakening its currency.
  • Foreign reserves: Central banks like the State Bank of Pakistan hold foreign currency reserves and sometimes intervene to stabilize the exchange rate.

Pakistan Remittances: Why Exchange Rates Matter So Much

Pakistan is one of the world's top remittance-receiving countries. Overseas Pakistanis sent home approximately $27 billion in remittances in the fiscal year 2023-24, making remittances one of Pakistan's largest sources of foreign exchange. [Source: State Bank of Pakistan]

For families receiving money from relatives in Saudi Arabia, UAE, UK, or the US, even a small change in the exchange rate makes a real difference. If the USD/PKR rate moves from 278 to 282, a $1,000 remittance is worth PKR 4,000 more. This is why checking live rates before transferring money matters.

Tip: Always check the live exchange rate before sending or receiving money. The rate your bank or exchange company offers may differ slightly from the interbank rate — this difference is called the spread, and it is how banks and exchange companies make money on currency conversions.

Interbank Rate vs Open Market Rate in Pakistan

In Pakistan, there are two main exchange rates you will encounter:

Interbank Rate

The rate at which banks buy and sell currency from each other. This is the official rate set by the State Bank of Pakistan and is used for large transactions, trade, and official remittances through banking channels.

Open Market Rate

The rate offered by currency exchange companies (sarrafas) on the street or in markets. This rate is usually slightly higher than the interbank rate for buying dollars and slightly lower for selling. The difference between the two is the exchange company's profit.

Our currency converter shows you the interbank reference rate — the most accurate and internationally recognized rate. [Source: State Bank of Pakistan]

Tips for Getting the Best Exchange Rate

Compare before you transfer

Different banks and exchange services offer different rates. Always check the live rate on our converter first, then compare what your bank or exchange service is offering. A difference of even 2-3 rupees per dollar on a large transfer makes a significant difference.

Avoid airport currency exchange

Airport currency counters typically offer the worst rates — sometimes 5-10% below the market rate. Exchange money before you travel or use an ATM at your destination.

Use official banking channels for remittances

Pakistan's State Bank offers incentives for remittances sent through official banking channels (banks and authorized exchange companies). Unofficial channels (hundi/hawala) are illegal and offer no recourse if something goes wrong.

Watch the trend, not just today's rate

If you regularly send or receive money internationally, track the exchange rate trend over a few days. Converting during a favorable rate can save significant money over time.
