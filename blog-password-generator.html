Why Weak Passwords Are So Dangerous

A weak password is not just a minor inconvenience — it is an open door to everything you keep online. Hackers use automated tools that can try millions of password combinations per second. A simple 6-character password can be cracked in less than one second by modern computers.

According to the Verizon Data Breach Investigations Report, over 80% of hacking-related breaches involve stolen or weak passwords. Weak passwords remain the single biggest security vulnerability for most people.

The most commonly used passwords worldwide are still shockingly simple. According to NordPass research, the top passwords used globally include "123456", "password", "123456789", and "qwerty" — passwords that take less than one second to crack.

What Makes a Password Strong?

A strong password has four key properties:

  • Length: Minimum 12 characters. 16 or more is ideal. Longer passwords are exponentially harder to crack.
  • Complexity: Mix of uppercase letters, lowercase letters, numbers, and symbols. Each type of character you add dramatically increases security.
  • Randomness: No dictionary words, names, dates, or predictable patterns. "P@ssw0rd" looks complex but is one of the first things hackers try.
  • Uniqueness: Different password for every account. If one site gets hacked and your password is leaked, hackers immediately try that same password on other sites.

Common Password Mistakes Pakistanis Make

Using personal information

Your name, your CNIC number, your date of birth, your phone number, your city — all of these are information that someone who knows you (or can find you online) already has. Never use personal information as a password. You can check your exact date of birth details using our Age Calculator — but make sure that information never ends up in your passwords.

Using the same password everywhere

This is the most dangerous habit. When any website you use gets hacked — and major data breaches happen constantly — your email and password combination gets sold on the dark web. Hackers then try that exact combination on Gmail, Facebook, bank apps, and every other site. One leaked password can compromise every account you own.

Writing passwords on paper or in WhatsApp

Many people in Pakistan store passwords by sending them to themselves on WhatsApp or writing them in a notes app. Both are insecure. Use a dedicated password manager instead.

Using simple number sequences

"123456", "111111", "000000" — these are the first combinations any hacker tries. They take milliseconds to crack.

What Is a Password Manager and Do You Need One?

A password manager is an app that stores all your passwords securely, encrypted behind one master password. You only need to remember one strong password, and the manager handles all the rest.

Popular free password managers include:

  • Bitwarden — free, open source, works on all devices. [bitwarden.com]
  • Google Password Manager — built into Chrome and Android, free and convenient.
  • Apple Keychain — built into iPhone and Mac, free.

The workflow is simple: use our Password Generator to create a strong random password, save it in your password manager, and never think about it again. [Source: NIST — National Institute of Standards and Technology]

Two-Factor Authentication: The Extra Layer

Even the strongest password can be compromised if a website's database is hacked. Two-factor authentication (2FA) adds a second layer of protection — usually a code sent to your phone — so that even if someone has your password, they cannot log in without also having your phone.

Enable 2FA on every account that offers it, especially your email, bank apps, and social media. [Source: CISA — Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency]
