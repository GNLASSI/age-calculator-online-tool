What Is a Percentage?

A percentage is a way of expressing a number as a fraction of 100. The word comes from the Latin "per centum" meaning "by the hundred." When you say 25%, you mean 25 out of every 100 — or one quarter of the total.

Percentages are used everywhere because they make it easy to compare numbers on a common scale. Saying "scored 67 out of 80" is less immediately meaningful than saying "scored 83.75%" — the percentage gives instant context. [Source: Math Is Fun]

The 4 Types of Percentage Calculations You Actually Need

Most percentage problems in real life fall into one of four categories. Our calculator handles all four:

1. What is X% of Y? (Percentage of a Number)

This is the most common type. You know the percentage and the total, and you want to find the actual value.

Formula: Result = (Percentage ÷ 100) × Number
Example: What is 20% of 5,000?
= (20 ÷ 100) × 5,000 = 1,000

Real life uses: calculating a discount amount, finding GST/tax on a price, calculating commission on sales, finding interest on a deposit.

2. X is What Percentage of Y?

You know both numbers and want to find what percentage one is of the other.

Formula: Percentage = (X ÷ Y) × 100
Example: 45 is what percentage of 180?
= (45 ÷ 180) × 100 = 25%

Real life uses: calculating your exam score as a percentage, finding what percentage of your salary you spend on rent, calculating attendance percentage.

3. Percentage Increase or Decrease

You have two values — an original and a new one — and want to know by what percentage things changed.

Formula: Change% = ((New Value - Original) ÷ Original) × 100
Example: Price went from PKR 800 to PKR 1,000
= ((1,000 - 800) ÷ 800) × 100 = 25% increase

Real life uses: calculating salary increase percentage, finding price inflation, measuring business growth, tracking weight loss or gain.

4. Add or Remove a Percentage

You want to add a percentage to a number (like adding GST) or remove a percentage from a number (like applying a discount).

Add: Result = Number + (Number × Percentage ÷ 100)
Remove: Result = Number - (Number × Percentage ÷ 100)
Example: PKR 10,000 + 18% GST = PKR 10,000 + 1,800 = PKR 11,800
Example: PKR 5,000 - 15% discount = PKR 5,000 - 750 = PKR 4,250

Common Percentage Mistakes to Avoid

Mistake 1: Confusing percentage points with percentages

If interest rates go from 10% to 12%, that is a 2 percentage point increase — but it is a 20% increase in the interest rate itself. These are two very different things and often cause confusion in financial news. [Source: Investopedia]

Mistake 2: Adding percentages incorrectly

If something increases by 50% and then decreases by 50%, you do not end up where you started. A PKR 1,000 item that goes up 50% becomes PKR 1,500. Then down 50% becomes PKR 750 — not PKR 1,000. Percentages are calculated on the current value, not the original.

Mistake 3: Reversing the percentage relationship

A 20% discount on a PKR 1,000 item saves PKR 200. But if you want to find the original price when the discounted price is PKR 800, you cannot simply add 20% to PKR 800 (that gives PKR 960, not PKR 1,000). You need to divide by 0.8 instead. Percentages in Finance and Business

In business and personal finance, percentage calculations are used constantly:

  • Profit margin: If you buy something for PKR 500 and sell for PKR 700, your profit is PKR 200 and your profit margin is 40% (200 ÷ 500 × 100).
  • GST/Sales Tax: Pakistan's standard GST rate is 17% on most goods. Adding 17% to a price before tax gives you the final price.
  • Bank interest: If your savings account offers 12% annual interest on PKR 100,000, you earn PKR 12,000 per year. You can also use our Loan Calculator to calculate loan interest.
  • Inflation tracking: If rice costs PKR 200 per kg this year and PKR 240 next year, that is a 20% price increase.
