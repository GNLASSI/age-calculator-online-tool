What Is a QR Code?

QR stands for Quick Response. A QR code is a two-dimensional barcode that stores information — a URL, a phone number, a WhatsApp message, an email address, or plain text — in a pattern of black and white squares. When you point a smartphone camera at it, the phone reads that pattern and instantly takes action: opening a website, starting a call, or sending a message.

QR codes were invented in 1994 by a Japanese company called Denso Wave, originally to track car parts during manufacturing. [Source: Denso Wave — QR Code History] What started as an industrial tool became one of the most widely used communication technologies in the world, used by billions of people daily.

According to Statista, QR code scans worldwide increased by over 750% between 2018 and 2023. The COVID-19 pandemic accelerated adoption dramatically as businesses replaced physical menus and forms with contactless QR alternatives.

How Does a QR Code Actually Work?

A QR code stores data using a grid of black and white squares. Each pattern of squares represents a specific piece of information in binary format — the same way computers store all data as ones and zeros. When your phone camera captures the image, software decodes the pattern and reads the stored information, then acts on it instantly.

QR codes can store up to around 3,000 characters of information. They also have built-in error correction, meaning even if part of the code is damaged or obscured — by a scratch, a sticker, or poor printing — the code can still be read correctly. [Source: ISO/IEC 18004 — QR Code Standard]

Most modern smartphones — both Android and iPhone — can scan QR codes directly through the built-in camera app, with no separate scanner app needed. [Source: Apple Support]

5 Real Ways People Use QR Codes Every Day

1. Sharing a Website Link Instantly

Instead of telling someone "go to my website and type this long URL," you put a QR code on your business card, flyer, or WhatsApp status. They scan it and land on your site in two seconds. This is the most common use case for bloggers, freelancers, and small business owners.

2. WhatsApp Business Contact

If you run a business on WhatsApp, a QR code that opens a WhatsApp chat with a pre-written message is incredibly useful. Print it on your packaging, your receipt, or your shop window. Customers scan it and your WhatsApp opens automatically — ready for them to ask questions or place an order.

WhatsApp itself uses QR codes to link the WhatsApp Web interface to your phone. [Source: WhatsApp FAQ]

3. Restaurant and Cafe Menus

Since 2020, QR code menus have become standard in restaurants worldwide. Instead of printing and laminating physical menus, restaurant owners put a QR code on each table that links to their online menu. It is cheaper, easier to update, and reduces physical contact. [Source: Nation's Restaurant News]

4. Sharing Contact Information

A QR code can store your full contact card — name, phone number, email, address, and website — in a format called vCard. When someone scans it, your details are added to their phone contacts automatically. No more typing out phone numbers by hand.

5. Event Tickets and Boarding Passes

Airlines, cinemas, concerts, and sports events all use QR codes on tickets. Staff scan the code at entry to verify the ticket instantly. Pakistan International Airlines (PIA) and major event organizers across Pakistan now use QR-based ticketing systems.

Tips for Using QR Codes Effectively

Always test before printing

Before you print 500 business cards or flyers with your QR code, scan it yourself on two different phones. Make sure it opens exactly where it should. A QR code with a typo in the URL is useless once it is printed.

Use a high resolution download for print

For printed materials, download the largest size available and print it at minimum 2cm x 2cm. Too small and some phones struggle to read it, especially in low light.

Add a call to action near the code

People are more likely to scan a QR code when they know what will happen. Write a short label next to it: "Scan to visit our website" or "Scan to WhatsApp us" or "Scan for the menu." It removes hesitation and increases scans.

Keep the destination page mobile friendly

People scan QR codes on their phones. If the page they land on does not work well on mobile — small text, broken layout, slow loading — they will leave immediately. Make sure your destination page is mobile optimized before linking a QR code to it.
