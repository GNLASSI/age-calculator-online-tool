How to Resize Images for WhatsApp

WhatsApp automatically compresses images when you send them, but this often results in blurry, pixelated photos — especially when you are sharing business graphics, product photos, or important documents.

The solution is to resize and optimize your image before sending it, so WhatsApp has less to compress. Here are the recommended sizes:

  • Profile photo: 500 x 500 px — square, kept under 500KB
  • Business catalogue photo: 800 x 800 px for product images
  • Shared documents/graphics: 1200px wide maximum — WhatsApp will still compress but less aggressively
  • WhatsApp Status image: 1080 x 1920 px (9:16 ratio, vertical)

WhatsApp compresses images to under 100KB automatically, which is why photos often look bad after sending. If you need to share an image without quality loss, use WhatsApp's "Document" option instead of the photo attachment — this sends the file without compression. [Source: WhatsApp FAQ]

How to Resize Images for Facebook and Instagram

Facebook and Instagram both compress uploaded images, but uploading the correctly sized image first gives you the best possible quality after their compression.

Facebook Best Practices

Facebook recommends uploading images in JPG format for photos and PNG for graphics with text. For posts, 1200 x 630 pixels is the standard that displays correctly across desktop and mobile without cropping. [Source: Facebook Help Center]

Instagram Best Practices

Instagram supports three aspect ratios: square (1:1), landscape (1.91:1), and portrait (4:5). For the best quality, upload at 1080px width in all cases — Instagram will handle the height based on your chosen ratio. [Source: Instagram Help Center]

KB vs MB: Understanding File Size

When government portals say "maximum 200KB", many people are confused about what this means. Here is a simple breakdown:

  • 1 KB (Kilobyte) = 1,024 bytes — very small, like a text message
  • 1 MB (Megabyte) = 1,024 KB — a typical compressed photo
  • Smartphone photo = usually 3MB to 15MB
  • 200KB limit = you need to reduce your photo to about 1/15th to 1/75th of its original size
