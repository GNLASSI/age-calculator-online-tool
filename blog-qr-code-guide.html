What Exactly Is a QR Code?

QR stands for Quick Response. A QR code is a two-dimensional barcode — a pattern of black and white squares — that stores information. When a smartphone camera points at it, the phone reads that pattern in milliseconds and takes action: opening a website, dialing a number, starting a WhatsApp chat, or displaying text.

QR codes were invented in 1994 by Denso Wave, a Japanese automotive company, originally to track car parts in manufacturing. [Source: Denso Wave — QR Code History] Three decades later, QR codes have become one of the most widely used communication technologies in the world — used by billions of people daily for everything from restaurant menus to airline boarding passes.

📊 According to Statista, QR code scans worldwide increased by over 750% between 2018 and 2023. The COVID-19 pandemic dramatically accelerated adoption as contactless solutions became essential for businesses everywhere.

Tips for Using QR Codes Effectively

Always test before printing

Before you print 500 business cards or 1000 flyers with your QR code, scan it yourself on two different phones — an Android and an iPhone if possible. Confirm it goes exactly where it should. A QR code with a typo in the URL is useless once it is printed and cannot be changed.

Print at the right size

For printed materials, your QR code should be at least 2cm x 2cm — ideally 3-4cm for most uses. Too small and some phones struggle to read it, especially in low light or when printed on textured paper. Download the largest size available and scale it in your design software.

Add a call to action

People are more likely to scan a QR code when they understand what will happen. Always write a short label next to it: "Scan to WhatsApp us", "Scan for our menu", or "Scan to visit our website." This simple addition can double your scan rate.

Keep the destination mobile-friendly

QR codes are scanned on phones. If the page your QR code links to looks broken on mobile, has tiny text, or loads slowly, visitors will leave immediately. Use our Image Resizer tool to optimize images on your target page before linking your QR code to it.

Use high contrast

Black QR code on white background works best and is most reliably scanned by all phones. Avoid low-contrast color combinations — light colors on light backgrounds or dark on dark are harder to scan and may fail on older phones.

QR Code vs Barcode: What is the Difference?

Traditional barcodes (the ones on product packaging) are one-dimensional — they store data in a series of vertical lines and can only hold about 20 characters. QR codes are two-dimensional — they store data in a grid of squares and can hold up to 3,000 characters of information.

QR codes also have built-in error correction. Even if up to 30% of the QR code is damaged, obscured, or covered by a logo, it can still be read correctly. This is why you often see company logos placed in the center of QR codes without affecting their function. [Source: ISO/IEC 18004 — QR Code Standard]
